--- title: I Built a 2-Player Brain Game at a Google Community Event (and Then Leveled It Up) published: false tags: ai, gamedev, javascript, beginners --- Last night I attended VIBE CODIING WITH GOOGLE AI STUDIO, hosted by GDGOC in PESHAWAR. The session was about Google AI Studio and how it can turn a rough idea into a working app in minutes. At the end, the speaker gave us a challenge: **build a game. The top 3 would get a surprise.** 🎁 Here's what I built, what I learned, and how I took it further. ## 🎮 Play it first {% codepen [https://codepen.io/editor/Salman-Tariq-the-sasster/pen/01a1465d-e154-79f0-9507-43c0fea83afb] %} **Controls:** Player 1 uses `A` `D` to move and `W` to fire. Player 2 uses the arrow keys. No friend nearby? Pick **Cosmic Bot**. ## 💡 The idea: Number Nebula I wanted a game that is **fun for kids but still makes adults think**. So I mixed a classic space shooter with puzzles: - **Math Duel:** solve the sum, then shoot the alien carrying the answer. - **Word Builder:** read a clue, then shoot letters in the right order to spell the word. - **Brain Mix:** both, taking turns. Two players share one screen, and every 3 correct answers in a row fires an attack (Freeze, Fog, or Meteor Rush) at your rival. ## 🔁 How I built it 1. **Prompt:** I wrote a structured prompt (concept, controls, levels, scoring, visuals) and gave it to Google AI Studio. It built a working version on the first try. 2. **Iterate:** I listed improvements and ranked them by impact. The biggest problem was that players never affected each other, so I added combo attacks. 3. **Level up:** I added power-ups, a boss battle, age-based difficulty (so a 7-year-old and an adult can play a fair match), a bot opponent, and the Word Builder mode. **My biggest lesson:** make one change at a time, test it, then move on. Small steps beat one giant request. ## ⚙️ Under the hood - Pure **HTML5 Canvas + JavaScript**, one single file, no frameworks - Sound effects generated in code with the **Web Audio API** (no audio files) - A simple **game loop**: update positions → check collisions → draw, about 60 times a second ## 🚀 What's next [e.g. online multiplayer, more word packs, a leaderboard]
